I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics in Garching, working on [FermiQP](https://www.quantum-munich.de/experiments/fermiqp.html), a fermionic quantum processor based on a lithium quantum gas microscope. Using spin- and charge-resolved imaging in optical lattices, we study strongly correlated fermions and programmable quantum dynamics at the single-atom level.

My current research focuses on doped Mott insulators – including emergent stripe correlations, magnetic polarons, and *t*–*t*′ Hubbard regimes – as well as multi-orbital lattice proposals such as the three-band Emery model for cuprates and nickelates. On the control and numerical side, I work on entropy-guided cooling and coherent unitary rearrangements in optical superlattices, alongside tensor-network and quantum Monte Carlo simulations to benchmark microscope data against theory.

Before moving to Munich, I completed my PhD with Saijun Wu in the [Ultracontrol Lab](https://ultracontrol.fudan.edu.cn/) at Fudan University, where I developed nanosecond and picosecond optical control of spinor matter waves, composite Raman pulse sequences, and geometric control of collective spontaneous emission.
