I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics in Garching, working on [FermiQP](https://www.quantum-munich.de/experiments/fermiqp.html), a fermionic quantum processor based on a lithium quantum gas microscope. Using spin- and charge-resolved imaging in optical lattices, we study strongly correlated fermions and programmable quantum dynamics at the single-atom level.

My current research explores emergent phenomena in strongly correlated matter, from the competition between stripes and *d*-wave pairing in the doped Fermi-Hubbard model to multi-orbital Emery models that capture key physics of cuprates and nickelates. Alongside experiment, I use tensor-network and quantum Monte Carlo simulations to benchmark data, guide experimental regimes, and design microscopic observables.

Before moving to Munich, I completed my PhD with Saijun Wu in the [Ultracontrol Lab](https://ultracontrol.fudan.edu.cn/) at Fudan University. Together with the team, we developed nanosecond and picosecond optical arbitrary waveform generators and used them to achieve ultrafast, precise control of Raman transitions for matter-wave interferometry and of optical dipole transitions for studying many-body quantum optics.
